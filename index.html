How it works

Confirming ovulation. A rise in basal body temperature happens after ovulation, so temperature alone cannot tell you in advance that you are fertile today. The app uses the sympto-thermal “3-over-6” rule: three consecutive readings above the highest of the previous six (the coverline), with the third at least 0.2 °C (about 0.4 °F) above it, or a fourth reading above it, and the rise has to hold (a lone spike that falls back is ignored). Ovulation is estimated as the day before the first high reading. Disturbed readings are ignored.

Predicting this morning. Each confirmed cycle teaches the app on which cycle day you tend to ovulate. The fertile window is estimated as the five days before ovulation through the day after (research on conception timing finds a roughly six-day window ending on ovulation day, with the two days before it the most fertile). With no history it falls back to a 28-day-cycle assumption, with a wide margin.

Same-day signals. Cervical mucus is the strongest same-morning sign: egg-white or watery mucus raises the estimate regardless of the calendar. After a confirmed temperature rise, the window is marked closed for that cycle.

Limits. Irregular cycles, PCOS, shift work, illness, some medications and travel can make any method less reliable. This app is not a medical device and not a substitute for medical advice. General guidance is to see a clinician if you have not conceived after 12 months of trying (6 months if you are 35 or older), or sooner if your cycles are very irregular or you have other concerns.
